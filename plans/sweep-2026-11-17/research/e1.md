Reading: the box's centre (1125.813, 70.188) and the ring's outer extents are identical at every
angle, paused and live — the element does not translate. The ink centroid, however, traces a circle of
radius ~1.17-1.18 CSS px (at DPR 1 and 2) around the box centre (8.0/8.25 ± 1.18), i.e. ~13% of the
9px diameter, once per 0.7 s revolution.

## Holds

Held with a different cause. Nothing is mis-positioned: there is no `transform` conflict (the busy
rule has no centring transform for `rotate()` to override), the box is square (9x9, 7x7 in a tile
footer), `transform-origin` is its centre, and the element is built once and never rebuilt per render
tick, so the animation does not restart. What moves is the glyph's visual mass: one whole quarter of
the ring is transparent, so the visible 270° arc's centroid sits ~1.2px off centre and orbits as it
spins. On a 9px glyph that reads as the spinner sliding around rather than turning in place.
Applies equally to the tile-footer 7x7 variant (same rule, same glyph).

## Cause

`web/src/style.css:1223-1228` — `.shellact[data-act="busy"]` draws the spinner as
`border: 1.5px solid var(--fg-dim); border-top-color: transparent;` and rotates the whole box with
`@keyframes shellact-spin` (`style.css:1252`). The glyph is rotationally asymmetric (three opaque
quarters, one empty), so its ink centroid is offset ~1.2px from the rotation centre and orbits it;
at this size that orbit is a large fraction of the glyph and is perceived as positional drift. The
fix is a rotationally balanced glyph, e.g. two opposite transparent quarters
(`border-top-color` and `border-bottom-color: transparent` — a dual-arc spinner, ink centroid exactly
on the centre, still only `--fg-dim`), or a faint full-ring track under one coloured arc.
