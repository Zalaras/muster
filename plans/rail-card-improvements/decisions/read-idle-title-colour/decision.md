# Decision: read-idle-title-colour

**Outcome**: A — match the mockup's ladder: add `color: var(--fg)` to `.card .name`, leaving the read-idle rule (`.card.s-idle:not(.unread) .name { color: var(--fg-muted) }`) as shipped. The drop from `--fg` to `--fg-muted` becomes real in the rail; the strip already rendered titles at `--fg`.
**Reached by**: consensus (advocate-b conceded in turn 1)
**Decisive argument**: The brief's premise that Option A brightens titles "in the rail and the strip" was measured false. Nothing between `body { color: var(--fg) }` and `.strip .card .name` declares a colour (advocate-a, turn 1: `.strip`, `.strip .card`, `.card`, `.card-in`, `.r1` all silent; advocate-b verified the same chain and that `#tiles-strip` is a sibling of the tiles grid, never inside `.cards`). So the one shared template already paints its title `--fg` in the strip and `--fg-dim` in the rail, and the shipped read-idle rule already drops correctly in the strip while rising in the rail. "A makes the two surfaces agree on the value one of them already ships; B fixes the rail by deleting a drop that currently works." Blast radius is one element: every other card descendant declares its own colour, and the only other inheritor of `.cards { color: var(--fg-dim) }` is the "No sessions yet" text node, untouched. `make contrast` gates token pairs, not selectors, and `--fg` on every card ground is already gated at or above 4.5:1; both advocates ran it on the shipped tree (three themes, 43 pairs each, 0 failures). design-system §2 gives the session title its own display role apart from metadata, so a rail title in the metadata token was the pre-existing defect REQ-9 exposed.
**Dissent to honour**: None on the outcome. advocate-b recorded two corrections worth carrying: the brief called `kb:adr/nongoal-accessibility-i18n-multiuser-other-platforms` accepted, and its frontmatter says `rejected` (nothing turned on it); and the history that raised the `--fg-dim` token in `ui-text-and-focus` is evidence nobody re-examined the title's token, not evidence anybody chose it.
