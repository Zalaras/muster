The diff on screen is out of date — how does the viewer say so?

The open diff does not redraw when the checkout changes; within about ten seconds it shows a notice with Refresh, and the lines stay put. Each frame below is scrolled part-way down, because a notice that lives only at the top of the scroll content would be off screen.

Mock state

A A bar across the top of the diff

A full-width amber bar pinned under the toolbar, with a filled Refresh at its right end; it slides down on arrival.

  • Good: hardest to miss, it stays put while the diff scrolls, and it says what changed.
  • Cost: it takes its own row, so the diff body shifts down by the bar's height (28px) when it arrives.

B The Refresh button and summary change in place

No new element: the toolbar's Refresh turns into a filled accent button with a dot and pulses twice, and the summary goes muted with an "out of date" tag.

  • Good: nothing moves and no new element is added.
  • Cost: it lives in the toolbar corner, so it is the easiest of the three to miss while reading lower down.

C A floating pill over the diff, plus dots on the changed files

A pill floats near the bottom of the diff body and stays put while it scrolls; changed files carry an amber dot and a new file appears as a muted row tagged new.

  • Good: it appears where the eye is, and shows which files changed before refreshing.
  • Cost: it covers a line or two of the diff, and the daemon has to say which files differ.
